Strategy

The fund decision behind every simulation on this page.

VOO or VTI: pick one core fund
The short version: for most investors, choose ONE core fund, VOO or VTI, and stick with it. Holding both mostly buys the same large companies twice.

Why one is enough. VTI holds the whole U.S. market, but it is weighted by company size, so the S&P 500 companies inside VOO make up roughly 80 to 85 percent of VTI by weight. The two funds move almost in lockstep (correlation near 0.99). Owning both adds a second ticker, a second tax lot list, and rebalancing chores, without meaningfully changing what you own.

FeatureVOO (S&P 500)VTI (Total Market)
ScopeTop ~500 U.S. companies~3,600+ U.S. companies
Expense ratio0.03%0.03%
Top holdingsApple, Microsoft, NVIDIA, etc.Nearly identical, slightly lower weights
CharacterLarge, established companies onlyAdds mid and small caps for completeness

Pick VOO if you want the large, profitable core of the U.S. market and nothing else. Pick VTI if owning everything, including small caps, helps you stay the course. Fees and tax efficiency are essentially the same; staying invested matters more than the choice between them.

The one exception: tax-loss harvesting

In a taxable brokerage account, the pair becomes useful. The funds track different indexes (S&P 500 versus CRSP US Total Market) while behaving very similarly, so if one drops you can sell it to realize a tax loss and immediately buy the other to stay invested. Most interpretations treat them as not substantially identical for the wash sale rule. That is a tax tool, not a diversification strategy.

Routing reminder: whichever fund you pick, new money still follows the routing in Plan: 401(k) room first, then IRAs, then taxable brokerage.

Return scenarios

  • Conservative 7%, Moderate 10%, and Historical 12.9% are fixed annual return assumptions for the deterministic paths. The 12.9% figure is a historical scenario carried from earlier versions of this tool; it is not a prediction, and no one can promise VOO or VTI will earn it again.
  • The accumulation engine invests contributions monthly and compounds monthly at the scenario rate divided by 12, depositing each month's contribution before that month's growth, matching the original tool.
  • Annual contributions grow each year by your contribution increase setting (0% keeps the original flat pattern).

Inflation and today's dollars

  • Today's dollars figures divide a future amount by (1 + inflation) raised to the years from today, using your inflation setting.
  • Retirement spending and guaranteed income start at the today's dollars amounts you entered and rise with inflation each year of retirement.

Retirement withdrawals

  • Each retirement year: net withdrawal equals that year's spending minus guaranteed income (Social Security plus other guaranteed income). The net amount leaves at the start of the year, then the remainder grows at the scenario return for the year. If guaranteed income exceeds spending, the surplus is added to the portfolio at the start of the year instead.
  • The market drop setting is applied once, before the first retirement year. A portfolio that cannot cover a year's net withdrawal is marked depleted at that age.

Monte Carlo

  • Annual returns are drawn from a normal distribution centered on your planning return with your volatility as the standard deviation, floored at -80% and capped at +100%. Within each year the drawn return is spread evenly across months while contributions continue monthly.
  • Success means the portfolio is never depleted before the plan-through age. Percentiles rank all paths, with depleted paths counted as zero at the plan-through age.

What is not modeled

  • Taxes on withdrawals or gains, fees beyond the funds' own expense ratios, Social Security claiming age adjustments, healthcare shocks, long term care, irregular large expenses, pensions that do not keep up with inflation, and changing your plan mid-course. If those matter to your household, enter their cost in spending or guaranteed income, or talk with a qualified professional.
